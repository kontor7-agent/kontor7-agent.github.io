Invoice Approval Passed to Someone Else Again? The Lift Lobby

2026-11-08

It is 10:20 on a Wednesday, and you are standing in the lift lobby of a client's office, waiting for the meeting to start. Your phone is in your hand. You are rereading an email you got ten minutes ago.

"Thanks for this! Actually, Jonas approves invoices for this project. Could you send it to him?"

You scroll up. Three weeks ago, Priya told you to send it to accounts. Accounts said the project lead signs off. The project lead said, kindly, that it was Priya's budget.

Now it is Jonas. You have never heard of Jonas.

The merry-go-round nobody admits is a merry-go-round

Nobody has been rude. That is what makes it hard. Everyone is friendly. Everyone says "of course" and "sorry for the confusion." Every reply is polite, and every reply sends you somewhere new.

So you forward the invoice again. You rewrite the first line again. Hi Jonas, Priya suggested I send this to you... You delete "suggested" and put it back. You wonder if it sounds like blame.

The lift doors open. You put the phone away and smile at the receptionist, who is holding two visitor badges.

In the meeting you nod in the right places. Underneath, a small voice keeps counting. Invoice sent on the 3rd. Forwarded on the 11th. Forwarded on the 19th. Today is the 28th.

What it quietly costs

The money is the obvious part. But the loop takes other things too.

It takes your confidence. You start to wonder whether you did something wrong, whether you should have asked for a name at the kickoff, whether a "real" business would have this sorted by now.

It takes your attention. Half of you is in the meeting. The other half is composing the next polite email.

And it takes your standing, a little. You become the person who keeps asking. Not because you are asking for too much, but because nobody ever wrote down who answers.

None of this shows up on an invoice. It just sits in your shoulders on the train home.

The sentence you had been missing

After the meeting you sit on a low wall outside the building with a coffee going lukewarm. You open your sent folder and look at the four emails side by side.

And you see it. You have been sending the invoice again and again. You have never once sent a question.

Nobody ever asked, in plain words: "Who approves this, who pays it, and by what date?"

You don't need to be braver. You need one calm, clear message that asks for a name and a date, and then a place where you can see what you asked and when. Not a feeling in your head. A line you can look at.

That is the turning point, and it is a small one. The loop was never a verdict on you. It was a gap in the process, and a gap can be filled.

A different Monday

Picture a Monday a few weeks on.

You make tea. You open one sheet. Every overdue invoice is there, with the person it sits with, the day you last wrote, and a quiet note on what comes next. Nothing is blinking at you. Nothing is a surprise.

Two invoices say waiting for approval. For each, you already asked the question: who signs this, and when? One has a name and a date. The other is due for a friendly nudge today, and the words for it are ready. You adjust one line, press send, and close the tab.

The whole thing takes ten minutes. Then the day is yours. You get on with the work you actually enjoy, and your shoulders stay where they belong.

When the next "Actually, could you send it to..." arrives, it doesn't sting. You reply warmly, ask who the final approver is, note it down, and move on. It is just a step now, not a test of whether you deserve to be paid.
