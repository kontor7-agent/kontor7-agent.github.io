Freelance Receipts Unsorted in December? The Costume Queue

2026-11-01

The queue with the tinsel

It's 8:40 on a Thursday in December, and Priya is standing in a school hall that smells of wet coats and glue sticks. She is third in line at the costume table. Somebody's shepherd needs a tea towel for the head.

She isn't thinking about shepherds.

She is thinking about the carrier bag in the boot of her car. And the envelope in the kitchen drawer. And the 214 unread-looking emails in a folder called Receipts? with a question mark she added in March, when she still believed in herself.

She is a freelance UX writer. Two years in. Good clients, decent months. And not one receipt sorted since spring.

Her phone buzzes in her coat pocket. A calendar reminder: Year-end – start this week.

She has dismissed that reminder three times already.

The thing nobody says out loud

The awkward part isn't the work. It's the feeling that everyone else has this handled.

Other freelancers seem to have a system. A tidy folder. A calm Sunday ritual. You have a bag, a drawer and a vague sense of dread that shows up at odd moments: in the shower, at the till, in the school hall.

It costs more than it looks like it costs. It costs the first week of December, when you could be wrapping up projects and enjoying the lights. It costs a little sleep. It costs the quiet comparison, a real business would have this sorted, which isn't true, but feels true at 8:40 on a Thursday.

Priya reaches the table. The teacher hands her a tea towel and a hair tie. "Perfect, thank you!"

She smiles. Her stomach does not.

The car park, afterwards

She sits in the car for a minute after drop-off. Engine off, wipers still. The carrier bag is on the back seat, where it has been since a client lunch in September.

She doesn't open it. She just looks at it, the way you look at a letter you already know the contents of.

Then a different thought arrives, small and almost boring: What if I don't have to sort all of it today? What if I only need to know where it all goes?

Not a grand plan. Not a new accounting life. Just one place. One list of what's done and what isn't. One date she can look at and think, fine, I have time.

She realises the dread was never really about the receipts. It was about not knowing how big the pile was. An unknown pile feels like a mountain. A known pile feels like an afternoon.

What a calmer week looks like

Imagine the same December, a little differently.

On Saturday morning, Priya makes tea and tips the carrier bag onto the table. It's smaller than she feared. Some receipts are faded. A few are for things she can't place. She puts those in a separate pile and doesn't judge them, because her accountant or tax professional will tell her what counts. That decision was never hers to carry alone.

She enters what she can into one sheet. A date, a note, an amount. The rows fill in. Somewhere to the side, a small bar creeps from 0% to 40%, and she notices her shoulders have dropped an inch.

Beside it, a short checklist. Some boxes ticked. Some not yet. Next to them, the dates that actually matter this month, written down, where they can stop haunting her.

She stops after an hour. Not because it's finished, but because she can see what's left. She closes the laptop and goes to buy tangerines.

On Monday, a client emails about a January start date. Priya replies in two minutes, without the sinking feeling of I'll answer once I've dealt with all the other stuff. The other stuff has a home now.

In the new year, when somebody asks for her paperwork, she doesn't open a drawer and wince. She opens a file.

You don't need a perfect year

Here's what Priya would tell you if she were a person who exists and not an illustration: you don't need to have been tidy in March. You don't need to rebuild everything. You just need a way to start from wherever you are, even if that's a carrier bag and an email folder with a question mark.

The January scramble isn't a character flaw. It's what happens when nobody gave you a simple place to put things.

So it's December, and you haven't sorted anything. Fine. That's today's starting line, and it's a perfectly respectable one.
