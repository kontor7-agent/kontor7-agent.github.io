Client Says They Paid, But You Can't Find It? The Ferry Terminal

2026-10-15

You are standing in the foyer of a ferry terminal, phone in one hand, a paper cup of coffee going cold in the other. Your sister's wedding weekend starts the moment the boat docks. The sea is flat and grey. The departure board flickers.

Then the email arrives.

"Hi, just to confirm, we paid this last Thursday. Can you check your side?"

You read it twice. Your stomach does the small drop you know too well.

Last Thursday. You open the banking app with a thumb that suddenly feels clumsy. You scroll. A grocery refund. A software subscription. A transfer from someone you can't place. Nothing from this client.

The thing you can't say out loud

Here is the part nobody tells you about being self-employed. It is not the money itself that makes you feel small. It is the doubt.

What if they did pay, and you simply missed it? What if the payment is there, hiding under a reference that says "invoice March" when you have sent three invoices to this client since March? What if you reply "I can't see it" and they send a screenshot, and you are the one who looks disorganised?

So you do what many of us do. You type a reply, delete it, type it again. You want to sound friendly, not accusing. You want to sound professional, not like someone guessing.

Behind you, a toddler is trying to climb the luggage trolley. A ferry horn sounds somewhere out in the harbour. You are supposed to be switching off for three days.

Two invoices, one amount

You remember now. This client had two invoices close together. Similar amounts, one a few euros apart. One was for the website copy, one for the follow-up round. You sent them eleven days apart, and you can't tell, standing here, which one was meant to be paid when.

Maybe the payment arrived under the other invoice. Maybe it was a part payment. Maybe it is stuck on their side in an approval queue, and "we paid" really means "it's scheduled."

You don't know. That is the whole problem. Not that something went wrong, but that you have no single place to look and say: this one is paid, this one is open, here is the date, here is the amount.

Instead you have a bank app, an email folder, two PDFs, and a vague memory. Your weekend is quietly leaking into all of it.

The reply you finally send

You send something short and careful: "Thanks for letting me know. I'll check and come back to you shortly."

It buys you time. It does not buy you peace.

On the boat you sit by the window and watch the coast slide away. You tell yourself you are not thinking about it. You are thinking about it. Every few minutes your mind drifts back to the same loop: which invoice, which amount, which day.

Later, at the dinner table, someone asks how work is going. You say "fine." It is the kind of fine that has a small knot inside it.

What you decide on the way home

On the return trip, with the sea going from silver to slate, something shifts. Not a grand resolution. Just a quiet, practical thought.

It doesn't have to feel like this.

The client did not do anything wrong. You did not do anything wrong. The only thing missing was a calm, simple overview: every invoice with its own number, its due date, and a clear mark for paid or not. Somewhere you could look in ten seconds instead of twenty minutes.

You don't need a complicated system. You need one page that tells the truth.

A different Monday

Picture the next time a client writes, "I think we paid that last week."

It is Monday, mid-morning. You have a tea beside the laptop. You open one file, find the client, and see two invoices side by side. One is marked paid on the ninth. One is still open, due on Friday.

You write back, easily: "Thanks! I can see the first invoice came through on the ninth. The second one is still open, due Friday. Could you check whether that one is in your queue?"

No apology. No guessing. No knot.

The client replies with a thumbs up and a "ah, yes, that one's still waiting for sign-off." The conversation takes three minutes. It feels like two professionals comparing notes, which is exactly what it is.

And your weekend, the next time you have one, stays yours.

If you recognise yourself

Mix-ups like this are rarely about dishonest clients or careless freelancers. They happen when the facts live in too many places. When the invoice numbers, the dates and the payments are scattered across a bank app, an inbox and your memory.
