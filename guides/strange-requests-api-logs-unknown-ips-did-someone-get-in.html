Strange API Log Requests, Did Someone Get In? The Shuttle

2026-11-07

The shuttle bus from the hotel to the conference centre takes eleven minutes. Jonas has been on it for four, and he has not looked out of the window once.

He is holding his phone low, between his knees, the way you hold something you don't want your neighbour to read. On the screen is a log excerpt he pulled up while brushing his teeth. Lines of it. Requests to /api/admin/users. Requests to /api/v1/export. A path ending in .env. All from IP addresses he has never seen, all at 03:12 and 03:13, all with a user agent that says nothing useful.

The man next to him is eating a croissant from a napkin. Jonas can smell the butter. His own stomach has closed like a fist.

Did someone get in?

The question you can't ask out loud

Jonas is a freelance developer. He built the booking API for a small physiotherapy group last spring, and he still looks after it for a monthly fee that is, honestly, a little low. There are no colleagues to turn to. There is no security team and no one to say, "Oh, that's just scanners, ignore it."

There is only him, a bus, and a talk on cloud costs that starts in nineteen minutes.

He scrolls. Most of the requests got a 404. Some got a 401. One got a 200, and he stops on it, thumb hovering. Then he realises it was the health-check endpoint. His own. His thumb relaxes a little.

But a little isn't enough. A 404 means nothing was there. It doesn't mean nothing was possible. He knows the difference, and that's the trouble. He knows just enough to know how much he can't be sure of.

The bus turns a corner. A woman in a lanyard laughs at something on her phone. Jonas types a message to the physio clinic's owner, deletes it, types it again, deletes it again. What would it even say? Hi, odd traffic, probably fine? Probably fine is not a sentence you send to someone whose patients' appointment times live in your database.

What it quietly costs

Here is what nobody puts in the invoice.

He will sit in the talk on cloud costs and take no notes. At the coffee break he will stand near the pastries, refreshing a log viewer on conference Wi-Fi. At lunch he will say "yes, busy, good" to a stranger and mean none of it.

The odd traffic itself may be nothing. The internet is full of things knocking on doors at three in the morning, rattling handles, moving on. Jonas knows this. But knowing it in general and knowing it about your own API are two different kinds of knowing, and only one of them lets you enjoy a croissant.

The cost is the day. A day he paid for, with a train ticket and a badge, spent half-present.

The turn, somewhere between stops

The bus stops outside the centre. Everyone shuffles toward the doors. Jonas stays in his seat for a second, letting them pass.

And he thinks something small and a bit uncomfortable: I have never actually gone through this thing properly.

He has fixed things. He has patched, tidied, added a check here and there when something occurred to him in the shower. But he has never sat down with a complete list and worked it through, item by item, writing down what he found. Every worry about this API has been answered from memory. And memory, at 03:12 on a Wednesday, is not much of a witness.

That's the realisation. The logs aren't the problem. Odd logs will always arrive. The problem is that he has nothing to hold them against. No list to say: this was checked, this wasn't, this is fine, this needs an afternoon.

He steps off the bus. The air smells of wet pavement and coffee. For the first time that morning, the fist loosens a notch, not because anything is solved, but because he has finally named what's missing.

A different Wednesday

Picture the same bus, a few weeks on.

Same logs, different morning. A burst of strange requests from unknown addresses, a handful of paths no honest client would ever try. Jonas sees them over breakfast and thinks: ah, there they are again.

Because now there is a document. He knows which doors exist, which ones need a key, and which keys went where. When he looks at a request for /api/admin/users, he doesn't wonder whether that door is open. He remembers checking it, and he can point to the line where he wrote it down.

He still reads the logs. He's not careless. But the reading has a floor under it. If something truly odd turned up, he'd know where to start, and he'd have something calm and organised to show the clinic's owner instead of a shrug.

The bus takes eleven minutes. He looks out of the window. There's a heron on the canal, standing in the shallows like it has all day.

At the break he eats the croissant. It's very good.
