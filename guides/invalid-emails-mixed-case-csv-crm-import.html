Invalid Emails in CSV Before CRM Import? Swim Lesson Wait

2026-11-05

It is 16:35 on a Wednesday, and you are parked outside the swimming pool.

Your daughter's lesson runs until 17:15. The laptop sits on the steering wheel, tilted at an angle your back will remember tomorrow. The heater ticks. Through the window you can see a row of parents on plastic chairs, all looking at their phones.

You are looking at a CSV file.

Imagine Jonas, a freelance web designer. He has just come back from a two-day trade fair where he collected 612 sign-ups on a tablet at his stand. Tonight he wants to put them into his new CRM, so the follow-up emails go out while people still remember his face.

He uploads the file. The CRM thinks for a moment. Then it answers.

Import finished. 241 rows skipped. Reason: invalid email address.

The list that looked fine

He had glanced at that file twice. It looked fine.

Then he opens it properly and the cracks show. Anna.Meyer@GMAIL.com sits above anna.meyer@gmail.com. Somebody typed info@firma,de with a comma where the dot should be. Someone else gave up halfway and left tom@outlook. A few addresses carry a trailing space you cannot see, only feel, because the CRM refuses them.

The names are no better. MARIA SCHULZ, peter brandt, Lena Vogt, all in the same column, all about to be greeted with "Dear MARIA SCHULZ" if he is careless.

He can feel what is coming. It is the small, tired thought every self-employed person knows: I will just fix these by hand.

What it quietly costs

Fixing by hand never takes the time you think it will. You scroll, you squint, you correct one row, and your eye catches another. You start to wonder whether the same person is in there twice, once in capitals and once not. Is it the same Anna? You are not sure, and you do not want to be the person who sends her two emails.

So you slow down. A job that should take ten minutes grows into the whole waiting hour, and then into the evening, because dinner, bath time and bedtime are not going to move.

The real cost is not the hour. It is the quiet embarrassment. These are people who stood at your stand and said yes, please write to me. Every day the list stays stuck in a file, the follow-up gets a little colder. And you know it. You are the one who promised yourself you would write on Wednesday.

There is a second worry nobody mentions. What if you fix it quickly, tired, in a car park, and break something? A good address deleted along with a bad one. A name changed that should have stayed. You would never know which.

So you either go slowly and lose the evening, or go fast and hope.

The door opens at 17:15

The pool door bangs open. Wet hair, a towel around the shoulders, a very hungry child.

"Can we get chips?"

Jonas closes the laptop with the list still broken, 241 rows still stuck. He says yes to the chips. In the queue at the snack bar, with a damp child leaning on his arm, he has the thought that changes the evening.

Why am I doing this by hand at all?

This is not the first messy export of his life. Last spring it was a client list. Before that, an event sign-up sheet. The file is different every time, but the mess is the same: capital letters in odd places, a stray space, a comma where a dot belongs. Every time he has treated it as a one-off crisis. It never was. It is just what lists look like when people type them on a tablet in a busy hall.

The mess is not the problem. Having no calm routine for it is the problem.

What a different Wednesday looks like

Picture the same afternoon, a little later in the story.

Jonas parks at 16:35, opens the file, and runs one cleaning pass before he touches the CRM. First he looks at a short report of what would change, so nothing happens behind his back. Then he lets it run. Mixed case is tidied. Spaces vanish. The addresses that can never work are set aside in a list of their own, where he can look at them in one glance instead of hunting through 612 rows.

He sees tom@outlook and info@firma,de sitting there, a handful of rows he can try to fix by hand, or simply let go.

He imports the clean file. The CRM says yes.

Then he closes the laptop, ten minutes into a forty-minute wait, and watches the lesson through the window. A small figure in a blue cap swims her length with one arm too high. He waves. She cannot see him.

The follow-up goes out that evening, after dinner, written calmly. Nobody is greeted in capitals.

That is all it is. No big transformation. A list that arrives in your CRM the way you meant it to, and an hour that stays yours.
