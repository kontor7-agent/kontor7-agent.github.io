Backup Folder Keeps Growing, Drive Almost Full? The Ferry Deck

2026-11-04

The ferry is halfway across when the notification arrives. You are standing on the open deck with a paper cup of tea, the wind pulling at your jacket, and your phone says: Backup drive almost full. 38 GB remaining.

You are on your way to a client workshop on the island. You are early. You are calm. And now there is a small cold stone in your stomach.

You know exactly what is on that drive. A folder called Backup. Inside it, folders called Backup_old, Backup_new, Backup_final and, somewhere further down, Backup_final_2. Nobody ever deleted anything. Why would you? Deleting a backup feels like the most dangerous thing you can do with a backup.

The folder that only ever grows

It began so sensibly. Two years ago you set up a nightly copy and felt, for the first time, like someone who ran a proper business. Client files, invoices, project folders, all safe.

Then the copies piled up. One a night. Each one nearly identical to the one before. Each one quietly taking its share of the drive.

You never decided to keep them all. You just never decided anything. The decision was always for later, for a week with more air in it.

So the drive filled the way a bath fills when you walk away. No drama. Just a slow line rising while you were busy with real work.

What it costs when nobody decides

The cost is not the drive. A new drive is easy to buy.

The cost is the small flinch every time you think about it. The knowledge that if the drive fills up, the backup might stop, and that you might not notice until the day you need it. The odd shame of being the person who recommends good habits to clients and cannot say how long her own copies are kept.

It is the Sunday you spent half an hour dragging old folders to the bin, then stopped because you were not sure which ones were safe to remove. You closed the window. You told yourself you would do it properly later.

Later is a very crowded place.

The question on the island

The workshop goes well. People are kind, the coffee is good, and at lunch someone mentions, in passing, that she keeps the last week of daily copies, a few monthly ones, and lets the rest go.

She says it the way you might say you keep milk in the fridge and not on the windowsill. Not clever. Not technical. Just a rule, decided once.

You nod and eat your sandwich. But the thought stays with you all afternoon, quiet and stubborn: you can just decide how long to keep things. Once. And then it happens by itself.

The old copies are not a treasure to guard. They are a stack that needs a sensible edge.

A different Monday

Picture the Monday after that.

You sit down with your coffee. The drive is not a worry today because the folder has a shape. Recent days are there in full. Older ones are thinned out to a few sensible points. Anything past its time has been cleared away without you lifting a finger.

There is a short logfile for last night's run. You open it, read three calm lines, and close it. Nothing surprising. No red bar. No cold stone.

If you ever wanted to see what a clean-up would touch before it touched anything, you could ask for a rehearsal first and read what it would do. No bravery required.

The drive has room. Your head has room too, which is the part you had not expected. The background hum of I should really sort that out simply stops, like a fridge you only notice once it goes quiet.

You start your first call of the week without a single browser tab about disk space open.

Why this is so easy to put off

It is easy to put off because nothing is on fire. A growing backup folder never sends an angry email. It just takes a little more of your trust each month.

And because backups feel serious, we treat the housekeeping around them as serious too. Better to leave it alone than to break something. So the cautious choice becomes the one that slowly causes the problem.

But caution and clutter are not the same thing. A good backup is one you can trust, and part of trust is knowing it will still be running next month, with space to do it.
