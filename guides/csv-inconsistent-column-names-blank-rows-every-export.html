CSV Column Names Change Every Export? The Park Bench

2026-10-17

It is 12:35 on a Wednesday, and Priya is on a park bench with a cheese sandwich she hasn't touched.

Her laptop is balanced on her knees. The sun is on the screen, which makes it hard to read. She doesn't need to read it, though. She knows exactly what she is looking at.

The weekly export has arrived again. Same shop, same system, same Wednesday.

Last week the column was called Customer Email. This week it is customer_email. Next week, she suspects, it will be E-Mail (Kunde), just to keep her humble.

And there are the blank rows. Three in a row after row 40. One more near the end. Another that looks empty but isn't, because something invisible is hiding in a cell.

Why does it do this every single time?

Priya is an illustrative character, a freelancer who keeps the back office running for a few small online shops.

The small thing that isn't small

Nobody warns you about this kind of annoyance. It isn't a disaster. Nothing is on fire. It is a file.

But it arrives every week, and it is never quite the same file. So you can never quite trust yesterday's fix.

Priya has a routine. She opens the CSV, renames the headers by hand, deletes the blank rows, scrolls to check she didn't miss one, and saves it under a new name. Then she saves it again under a slightly different name, because she isn't sure the first one was right.

It takes twenty minutes on a good day. On a bad day it takes an hour and ends with a number that looks slightly off. She can't say why, and she doesn't love not being able to say.

That's the quiet cost. It isn't only the time. It's the low hum of doubt afterwards.

What it costs when nobody is looking

The client call is at two.

Priya has promised a clean summary of last week's orders. Easy, in theory. In practice, she is now eating her sandwich in small, distracted bites while she scrolls for the fourth blank row.

She thinks about the time she sent a summary with a header that didn't match. The client was kind about it. That was almost worse.

She thinks about how she charges by the project, not the hour. Every hour she spends nudging columns into place is an hour she quietly gives away.

And she thinks, a little guiltily, about the lunch break. She had planned to read. Instead she is doing the same repair she did last Wednesday, and the Wednesday before that.

It makes you feel like you're not running a business. You're just holding one together with careful clicking.

The sentence she hadn't said out loud

A man walks past with a dog that has a stick far too big for it. Priya watches them go by and, for no reason at all, says it to herself.

I shouldn't have to do this by hand every week.

It sounds obvious. But she has been treating it as part of the job, like weather. The file changes, so she changes it back. That's just how exports are.

Except the file isn't random. It is annoying in the same few ways, over and over. Different names for the same column. Empty rows in the same places. The same small mess, dressed up differently each week.

And a mess that repeats is something you can stop meeting fresh every Wednesday.

That is the whole turning point. No big revelation. Just a sandwich, a bench, and the thought that she is allowed to want this to be easier.

What a normal Wednesday could feel like

Picture the same bench, a week from now.

The export lands. Priya opens it and doesn't tense up. The headers line up with what she expects, whatever they were called this time. The blank rows are gone. She also gets a short note of what was changed, so she doesn't have to wonder whether something slipped past her.

She checks it, nods, and moves on.

The sandwich gets eaten while it is still pleasant. She reads two pages of her book. At two o'clock she joins the call with the summary already done, and she is not rehearsing apologies in her head.

Nothing dramatic. Just a Wednesday that belongs to her again.

That is what we mean by relief. It isn't excitement. It's the knot in your shoulders letting go because the thing you dread has become boring.

If you recognise the bench

Maybe your file isn't a shop export. Maybe it's a bank download, a time-tracking report, or a list from a client who sends it differently every month. The details change. The feeling doesn't.

If you read this and thought that's me, with the renaming and the scrolling, you're not doing it wrong. You've just been doing a repeating job by hand.
