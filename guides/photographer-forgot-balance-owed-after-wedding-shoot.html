Forgot the Balance After a Wedding Shoot? The Physio Corridor

2026-10-15

It's 8:20 on a Thursday morning, and you're sitting on a plastic chair in a physio corridor. Your shoulder is complaining again. Too many hours holding a camera above the crowd.

You open Instagram to pass the time. There is a red dot on the notifications.

You were tagged in a post.

It's Hanna and Jonas. The wedding in June. A black-and-white shot of them laughing on the stone steps, the one you nearly didn't take because the light was going. Four hundred likes. A caption: Still can't believe how beautiful our day was. Thank you, @you.

Your chest goes warm for about two seconds. Then something else drops in underneath it.

The balance.

The number you didn't want to look at

You remember the deposit. It arrived in February, and you felt rich for an afternoon. You remember the gallery going live in July, the happy message they sent back, the three exclamation marks.

You do not remember an invoice for the rest.

Or maybe you sent one. Maybe it's sitting in a folder called Hanna & Jonas – FINAL. Maybe it went out the week your sister's flat flooded and you answered nothing for ten days. Maybe it never left your drafts.

The physio calls a name that isn't yours. You stay in the chair with your thumb hovering over the photo, doing sums you don't want to do.

It's October. If the balance is still open, that's three months. And the awkward part is not the money itself. It's that they love you. They tagged you. How do you write by the way, did you pay me? under a post that says thank you?

What the silence quietly costs

Nobody warns you about this part of the job. The camera work is the easy bit. It's the in-between that wears you down.

The small knot you carry about who has paid and who hasn't. The way you avoid opening one spreadsheet because you're afraid of what it says. The tab with the booking calendar, the tab with the invoices, the notes app with Maria, 2nd payment?? that you wrote at a train station and never looked at again.

You also carry the quiet embarrassment. You photograph other people's most important day with total focus, and then you can't tell whether your own bank account got its share.

So you do what many of us do. You say nothing, you tell yourself it's probably fine, and you take on the next shoot.

The message you finally send

After the appointment, you sit in the car with the engine off. You scroll through your bank app, slowly, month by month, back to June.

There it is. Not a payment from Jonas. Just the deposit, and then nothing under their names.

You open a blank message. You write it three times. The first version sounds like an apology. The second sounds like a lawyer. The third just sounds like you:

Hi both. I loved seeing the steps photo today, thank you for tagging me. While I was looking at the file, I noticed I never sent you the final invoice for the wedding balance. That's on me. I'll attach it here. Sorry for the delay, and I hope married life is treating you well.

You read it once and press send before your courage leaves.

Twenty minutes later, a reply: Oh no, we thought it was all settled! Paying tonight. So sorry!

It was nobody's villainy. It was a gap. A gap that sat there for three months because nothing in your week was built to notice it.

The part where you realise it doesn't have to be like this

The money lands the next morning. You should feel relieved, and you do, but there's something underneath that feels more like a decision.

The problem was never Hanna and Jonas. It was that your whole business lived in your head, a handful of folders and a good memory. A good memory is a lovely thing until the week it isn't.

What you want is not complicated. One place where each client lives. The shoot date, the deposit, what's still to come, when the gallery is due, and whether the final invoice has gone out. A single glance that says this one is done, this one is waiting, this one needs a nudge.

Not a system that runs your life. Just a quiet page that remembers, so you don't have to.

A normal Monday, afterwards

Imagine the next Monday morning. Coffee, a window with some real light in it. You open one sheet instead of four tabs.

Two shoots this month. One deposit in, one still pending, and a small flag next to the engagement session from last week: gallery due Friday. Under the October wedding, the balance sits in plain view, with the date it was invoiced beside it.

Nothing is on fire. Nothing is hiding. You spend ten minutes looking, one reminder is worth sending, and you send it in a friendly voice because you're not frightened of the answer.

Then you close the laptop and go and edit the photos you actually love editing.

The next time a happy couple tags you, you can enjoy it fully. Four hundred likes, a thank-you note, and no knot underneath.
