Same Data Chores Every Week? The Swim Lesson Gallery

2026-10-16

It is 10:20 on a Saturday, and the viewing gallery of the swimming pool smells of chlorine and wet towels.

Below you, your daughter is holding a float and trying very hard not to look at you. You wave. She doesn't wave back. That is fine. That is eight years old.

On your knees sits the laptop you promised yourself you wouldn't bring.

A client wrote at 8:05: Could you quickly look at the export again? Some rows look odd. Quickly. You know that word. It never is.

The three little jobs that are never one job

The export is the same mess as last month. Dates in two formats. Names with stray spaces. A few rows that appear twice, as if the file wanted to be sure you noticed them.

So you open the folder where your scripts live. You are fairly sure you wrote something for this. Somewhere. In spring.

There is clean_v2.py. There is clean_final.py. There is clean_final_REAL.py. You open the last one and it wants a column name that no longer exists.

The heat of the gallery presses on your neck. A parent beside you eats a sandwich out of foil. Somebody's little brother is hitting the glass.

Then the second thought arrives, as it always does. The client's attachments are a hundred files with names like scan (3) copy.pdf, and they need to land in tidy folders before Monday.

And right behind it, the third, the quiet one. A teammate asked on Thursday whether the little API you built had been looked over properly before it goes live. You said yes. You think you said yes. You meant to go through it once more.

Three small jobs. Each one is easy on its own. Together they are your whole Saturday.

What it quietly costs

Nobody sees this part of freelance and small-team work. It isn't the big project. It is the tax you pay around the project.

It is the hour of rewriting something you've already written. It is the afternoon that disappears into one stubborn file. It is the low hum of doubt: did I check that? Did I miss something?

And it is the way you are in a swimming gallery, physically present, and mentally in a spreadsheet.

You look up. Your daughter has let go of the float. She is swimming, properly, for four whole strokes. You miss the first two.

That's the moment something shifts. Not a grand realisation. Just a flat, clear thought: this isn't a skills problem. I know how to do all of this. I just keep starting from zero.

Starting from somewhere

You close the laptop, and for the rest of the lesson you watch the water. All of it.

On the way home she talks about the diving ring she almost caught. You listen, and your head is quiet for the first time in days.

Later, with the kettle going, you think about what you actually want. Not another clever script. You have plenty of those, scattered like coins in coat pockets. You want the chores to have a home. One place where the data cleaning lives, one for the file chores, one for looking over an API before it meets the world. Written to be reused, so that a messy CSV is a small thing you handle rather than a mood that takes over your Saturday.

That is all a simple system changes. It doesn't make the work vanish. It makes the starting line close.

A different Saturday

Picture the same gallery, a few weeks on.

The client's message comes in at 8:05. You read it over breakfast. You think, right, that again, and not oh no.

You know where the cleaning tools are. You run them on the export on Monday morning at your desk, with coffee, in the time it takes to drink it. The files get their proper names and their proper folders. The API gets its calm once-over before Thursday's release, not a guilty one at midnight after it.

Saturday at 10:20, the laptop stays at home.

You are on the bench, damp air on your face, watching a small person let go of the float and swim to the wall. You see every stroke. She looks up and, this time, she waves.

That's the whole picture. Not a bigger business or a cleverer stack. Just a weekend that belongs to you again.
