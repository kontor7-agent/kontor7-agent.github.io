Excel Trackers Scattered? The Climbing Gym Café Lunch

2026-10-15

It is Wednesday, 12:30, and you are sitting in the café of a climbing gym.

You are not here to climb. The gym has decent coffee and a table by the window, and it is the one place where nobody asks you anything. Your laptop is open next to a half-eaten cheese toastie. Through the glass, a kid in a bright green shirt is stuck halfway up a wall, laughing about it.

You are trying to answer one question from a client: "Can you send me an overview of what we've done so far and what's still open?"

It is a normal question. It should take two minutes.

Five files, one question

You open the invoice file. That one is fine. Then you remember the hours live somewhere else, so you open that one. The quote you sent in spring is in a third file, with a name that made sense at the time: quotes_new_FINAL. The payment reminders you meant to keep track of are in a fourth, last touched in June.

The fifth is a template you downloaded once, filled in for two weeks, and quietly abandoned.

Each file was a good idea on the day you made it. Each one solved one small problem. Together they have become a small town you have to navigate every time someone asks you something simple.

You take a bite of toastie and realise you are copying numbers from one window into another. The cheese has gone cold. The client is waiting.

The part nobody says out loud

The annoying thing is not the time. It is what the scattered files whisper while you work.

A real business would have this in one place.

You should know this by heart.

What if you missed something?

You don't miss much, usually. But you never fully trust it either. So there is always a low hum behind your day, the sense that somewhere in those five files is a number you haven't looked at in a while.

And it quietly costs you. A Sunday evening spent "getting organised" that was really just reopening everything. A reminder sent two weeks late because the file that would have prompted you was closed. A moment on a call where you say "let me get back to you on that" when you wished you could simply say the answer.

You have also, if you are honest, bought a thing or two along the way. A template here, a tracker there. Each one reasonable on its own. None of them talking to the others, none of them ever feeling like the finished picture.

The kid on the wall

By the window, the kid in the green shirt has made it down. An instructor is crouched next to him, not pointing out everything he did wrong, just showing him where to put his foot next time. One thing. The next hold.

You watch that for a moment, and something small clicks.

You have been treating your admin like a wall you need to climb in one go, with a different rope for each section. What you actually want is far more modest. You want to open one place, see the whole picture, and close it again.

Not a bigger system. Not more discipline. Just things that belong together, living together.

It doesn't have to be five files and a good memory. It can be a set of trackers that were built to sit side by side: the money owed, the hours worked, the quotes sent, the receipts waiting, all in the same calm style, all ready to use.

What a normal Wednesday could feel like

Picture the same table, the same window, the same toastie. This time the client's message arrives and you don't flinch.

You open one folder. You know where everything is, because it has always been there. Within a couple of minutes you have your answer, and you write it in a relaxed, ordinary voice. Here's where we are. This part is done, this part is open, this is what's next.

You press send, and the toastie is still warm.

There is a different quality to that kind of day. You are not hunting. You are not apologising for being slow. You are not carrying a vague worry around the climbing gym like a rucksack. You finish lunch, you close the laptop, and the afternoon is actually yours.

The same goes for Monday mornings, when the week ahead looks like a list rather than a fog. And for the January receipts moment, which turns into an ordinary evening instead of a small crisis.

None of this makes you a different person. It just removes the friction that made you feel like you were always slightly behind.
