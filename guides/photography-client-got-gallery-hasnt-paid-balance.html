Gallery Delivered, Balance Unpaid? The Barber's Mirror

2026-10-25

The cape, the mirror and the phone under it

The cape is snapped around your neck. The stylist is combing out the damp ends and asking, kindly, how work has been.

"Busy," you say. It is true.

Under the cape, your phone sits on your knee. You tilt it just enough to see the screen. The gallery dashboard says the family opened your link on Friday. Then again on Saturday. Then again last night.

There is a little heart next to forty-odd photos. Someone has been picking favourites. Someone has been downloading them.

The balance invoice went out eleven days ago. It is still marked open.

You look at yourself in the mirror. A woman with wet hair and a very calm face, doing arithmetic in her head.

The kind of silence that has pictures in it

This is a strange kind of unpaid.

The client is not rude. The client is not hiding. She sent a lovely message the day the gallery arrived: We cried a bit. Thank you so much. You printed that line in your mind and kept it.

So what do you do with a client who loves the work and has not paid for it?

You cannot write Where is my money? to someone who cried over your photographs. It feels wrong, like asking for a tip after a hug.

But the shoot took a Saturday. The editing took three evenings. The second-shooter fee left your account weeks ago. And the rent does not care how moving the pictures were.

"Lovely day for it," the stylist says, about nothing.

"Mm," you say, still doing the arithmetic.

What you don't say out loud

Here is the part you would never tell the stylist.

You are not completely sure what is owed. The deposit came in back in spring, and you think it was a third. Or was it a quarter? The package changed once. She added an extra hour at the park. That went into an email somewhere.

So even before you chase, you worry about chasing the wrong number. Asking for too little feels like a gift you never meant to give. Asking for too much feels like an accusation.

And there is something quieter underneath. You handed over every file in full resolution, on the very first day, because it felt generous. Because you wanted her to have them. Because that is why you do this.

Now the files are on her phone, and the invoice is on yours. You feel a little foolish, and a little unprofessional. You wonder whether other photographers have a system and you are the only one winging it.

Most of them are winging it too. Nobody talks about it in the group chats.

The turning point comes with a hairdryer

The dryer starts. For ten minutes there is nothing to do but sit.

You open your notes instead of your inbox. You try to find the shoot. It is under Tuesday ideas, Park family, and a voice memo called new recording 14. The deposit is in your bank app. The agreed package is in an email. The gallery date is in your head, and the invoice is in a PDF.

Four places for one client. Of course it feels like fog.

And you have a small, clear thought, not dramatic: this is not a client problem. This is a where-is-everything problem.

If you could see the whole job on one line, with the client, the shoot date, the deposit, the gallery and the balance, you would not be squinting at the mirror. You would know the number. You would know how many days it has been. You could write one calm sentence, send it, and let it go.

No begging. No guessing. Just a polite note with the right amount in it.

The dryer stops. The stylist holds up a small mirror to show you the back.

"Perfect," you say. You mean the haircut. You also mean something else, because for the first time all morning you feel a little lighter.

A different Monday

Picture the Monday after this one.

You open a single sheet with your coffee. Every shoot you have is there, in order. Two are waiting on a deposit. One gallery goes out on Thursday. One balance is nine days past due, and it sits quietly in its own colour, saying so without any drama.

You do not hold your breath while you look at it. You just know.

You write your note in two minutes, because the numbers are already in front of you. It sounds like you: warm, clear, professional. Hi Maria, a quick reminder that the final balance for the park session is still open. Here is the amount and the link whenever it suits you.

You press send. You close the laptop. You go and photograph a bowl of lemons for no reason, because your head is free.

That is all the system changes. It does not make clients pay faster by magic. It makes you the kind of business that always knows where each job stands, and that steadiness shows in how you write.
