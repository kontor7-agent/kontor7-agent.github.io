Another Year Gone, Freelance Going Nowhere? The Clock Shop

2026-11-01

Saturday, 10:20, a shop that smells of oil

The bell over the door rings. You are here to pick up your grandfather's watch.

It has been in the drawer for three years. In October you took it out and thought: I should at least get this fixed. It felt like a small, doable thing.

The clockmaker is old and unhurried. Wall clocks tick around him, none of them quite in step. He goes into the back room.

You stand there with your phone in your hand. You are not reading anything. You are doing the other thing, the thing you have done every week since October.

You are counting.

Ten months of freelancing. Or three years. Or eight. The number changes, but the question is the same. Where did this year go? Why am I exactly where I was last January?

The quiet arithmetic

Nobody asks you this out loud. Your sister asks how work is going and you say "busy, good." Both words are true and neither answers her.

But you know what you run on. A rough sense that you worked a lot. A vague memory of a good spring. A summer that went soft. An autumn when you chased two invoices and lost a week to a pitch that never came back.

If someone asked you to show them the year, you couldn't. You could only describe a feeling, and the feeling is that it went nowhere.

That feeling costs more than it looks like it costs. It makes you shrink your rates a little, because who are you to charge more? It makes you say yes to the wrong client, because maybe this one is the turn. It makes the empty weeks of December feel like a verdict.

What the clockmaker said

He comes back with the watch in a small paper bag, and he is almost apologetic.

"It wasn't broken," he says. "Dust, mostly. And it was running a little slow. Four minutes a day, maybe. Nobody notices four minutes. But after a month you're two hours late for everything, and you think the watch has stopped."

He taps the glass.

"It never stopped. It just wasn't being looked at."

You laugh, politely, and pay. Then you stand outside on the pavement with the bag in your hand and the sentence stays with you.

It never stopped. It just wasn't being looked at.

Because think about your year again. You did land clients. You did finish projects. Somebody paid you, and more than once. You learned a tool, or a way of talking to people, or which kind of job to turn down.

But none of it was written anywhere. There was no page that showed the shape of the months. So all of it blurred into one grey word: nowhere.

The turn

Maybe the year didn't fail. Maybe it just had no face.

That is a different problem, and a much kinder one. A year that went nowhere needs a verdict. A year nobody looked at only needs a place to be looked at, week by week, in small honest pieces.

Not a big reinvention. No five-year vision board. Just somewhere to write down who you worked for, what you sent, what came in, and what the week looked like. And a view of the whole year that you can hold in your two hands.

You don't need a dramatic system for that. You need a quiet one that is still there on a Thursday in March when you're tired.

A Monday in February, later

Picture it.

It is 8:40. The coffee is hot, because you actually drank it hot. A paper page lies open on the desk, one week, seven days, a little space for the three things that matter.

You can see that last week you billed two clients and one invoice is still open. You know which one. You don't feel the knot, because it is written down and you know the date you'll follow up.

Turn back a few pages and the month is there. It is not glamorous. But there are weeks in it, and you can see where they went. A slow stretch looks like a slow stretch, not like a personal failure.

When your sister asks how work is going, you have an answer that isn't a fog. "Steady. March is fuller than February."

That is all. It is not a transformation. It is the feeling of a watch that has been wound and is running on time.
