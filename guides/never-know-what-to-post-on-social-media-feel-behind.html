Never Know What to Post? The Wednesday Tram Ride Behind Again

2026-10-13

It's Wednesday, 8:12 am, and the tram is full of wet coats.

You're wedged between a pushchair and a man eating a pretzel. One hand holds the rail. The other holds your phone, thumb hovering over a blank caption box.

Your last post was last Thursday. Or was it the Thursday before?

You scroll up to check. It was the Thursday before.

There it is again, that small, familiar drop in the stomach. I'm behind.

The week that was supposed to be different

On Sunday you promised yourself a plan. You'd sit down with a coffee, decide the week's posts, write them, be done.

Then a client sent revised files. Then the washing machine beeped. Then it was Sunday night and the plan was a half-written note on your phone that says: tips? behind the scenes? that thing about process??

Now it's Wednesday, and the question follows you around like a kid asking for snacks. What should I post today?

You're a photographer, maybe. Or a copywriter, a coach, a one-person design studio. It doesn't matter. The feeling is the same. Everyone else seems to have a rhythm, and you have a feed that goes quiet, then bursts, then goes quiet again.

What it quietly costs

Nobody tells you that not knowing what to post isn't really about posts.

It's the ten minutes at lunch spent scrolling other people's accounts, feeling smaller with each swipe. It's the idea you had in the shower on Monday that is gone by Tuesday. It's the photo from last month's project sitting in a folder, still unshared, because you couldn't decide where it belonged.

It's also the small voice that says a real business would have this sorted. Real businesses have a plan. You have a phone full of fragments.

And the cost isn't dramatic. That's why it lingers. A prospect looks you up before writing, sees a feed that stopped in September, and quietly moves on. Maybe. You'll never know. That not-knowing is part of the weight.

The pretzel man gets off at Marktplatz

The tram empties a little. You find a seat and, instead of opening the caption box again, you do something odd.

You open your photo roll and look at the last two weeks as if they were someone else's.

There's the whiteboard from the kickoff call, covered in your handwriting. There's a screenshot of a kind message you won't share but that reminded you why you like this work. There's a half-finished sketch. There's a coffee cup next to a laptop, and a note: the client asked me this three times this month.

That's four posts. Not brilliant ones. Honest ones. They were never missing. They just had no place to land.

You realise the problem was never a shortage of things to say. It was that every day you started from zero, asking an empty page to come up with everything at once.

The turning point is smaller than you think

Someone once described it to you like this: you don't need more inspiration, you need a place where the ideas can wait.

A calendar, nothing more. A simple view of the month where Thursday already has a name on it. Where the whiteboard photo sits under Behind the scenes, the client question sits under Helpful tip, and the project result has its own square for next week.

Not a rigid schedule. Just a shelf, so that on Wednesday morning you aren't building the whole house.

It's a modest idea. But it's the first one in weeks that makes your shoulders drop an inch.

A different Monday

Picture it a few weeks from now.

Monday, 9:05. The kettle is on. You open one file and see the week laid out: a post planned for Tuesday, one for Thursday, a rough idea parked for the weekend. The captions are half-written. A few hashtags are waiting in a list, so you aren't hunting for them again.

You change one thing, because a client delivered early and the news is better than what you'd planned. That takes four minutes.

Then you close it and get on with the actual work.

On the tram that Wednesday, nothing nags at you. Your phone is just a phone. The pretzel man is back, and you almost smile at him.

Some weeks will still go sideways. A sick child, a deadline, a rainy fortnight. But a skipped Thursday becomes a skipped Thursday, not proof that you're not a real business. You pick it up again on Friday, because the plan is still there, patient and unbothered.

That's the whole change. Not more posting. Less dread about it.
