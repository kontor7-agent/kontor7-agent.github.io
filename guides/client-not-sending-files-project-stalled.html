Client Not Sending Files? The Sauna Bench Question

2026-10-27

Jonas has booked one hour at the lakeside sauna. It is the first thing he has done for himself in three weeks.

It is Wednesday, 14:20. He sits on the top bench with the heat pressing on his shoulders. His phone is in a locker outside, and he can still see the screen.

The shared folder is empty. It was empty on Monday and again on Tuesday morning. He opened it twice before lunch today, as if files might have arrived while he looked away.

The website for the physio practice goes live on Friday. He has the layout and the colours. He has a lovely, calm homepage with a big grey box where the team photo should be and the words Text follows where the text should be.

The client is friendly. That is the hard part. She said "Of course, this week, no problem" on the kickoff call, and she meant it. She is also treating patients from eight to six.

The Sentence You Keep Rewriting

Sitting in the heat, Jonas drafts the email again. Just a gentle reminder. No, too soft. As discussed, I still need. No, too stiff. I'm sorry to bother you again. Why is he apologising for asking for the thing the project depends on?

He has been through this before. He knows how the next two days will go.

Thursday, he will send the nudge. Nothing will come back. By Thursday night he will be tempted to write the texts himself, or to pull stock photos, or to quietly work the weekend. Then on Monday, the client will say the texts aren't quite right. The files will turn up, a day late and in the wrong format. The invoice will wait for "after the launch", and the launch will wait for her.

Nobody is being unfair. Still, he is the one holding the delay.

What bothers him most is the small, private suspicion underneath it all. A real professional wouldn't be in this spot.

What Was Never Said Out Loud

He steps outside and cools off on the jetty. The water is flat and grey. A man in a bathrobe nods at him.

He thinks back to the kickoff call. It was a good call, friendly and full of enthusiasm. At the end he said, "So I'll just need the content from you." She said, "Sure!"

Neither of them said which content. Neither said how many photos, in what form, or by what day. There was no "and if it isn't here by Tuesday, the launch moves to the following week." Nobody asked who else needed to approve the text, or whether the domain login was in her hands or her nephew's.

None of it was said, because none of it felt polite to say. He didn't want to sound like a clipboard.

Standing there in his towel, he sees it differently. The silence in the folder isn't a character flaw in his client. It is a gap at the start of the project, the part where two busy people nodded at each other and each assumed the other had it covered.

Someone could have closed that gap in ten minutes. He was just never given the sheet to do it with.

The Monday That Could Have Been

On the walk back to the car, he lets himself picture another version of the same project.

In it, the first week begins with one short, friendly page the client fills in. It asks what they are sending, who approves it and when it is due. The kickoff call follows a simple agenda, and everyone leaves knowing the next step. A scope note goes out afterwards in plain words. Access to the domain and the accounts is sorted before day three, not discovered on day nine.

In that version, the email on Thursday doesn't feel like begging. It feels like a date they both agreed to.

It is not about being strict. It is about being clear early, so the awkward email never has to exist.

He pictures an ordinary Monday inside that. The coffee is hot, and the folder already holds half of what he needs. The other half is marked "due Wednesday" in a document the client has seen and nodded at. He opens the laptop with a small, quiet sense of I know where this stands. He closes it at six.

Friday, Still Coming

The photos arrive Thursday at noon. Most are fine, and two are blurry. The text comes on Friday afternoon as a voice message, since she wrote it between patients. The launch moves to the following Wednesday. Jonas is fine about it, mostly.

What he keeps from that week isn't the delay. It is the question on the sauna bench: why did I have to start a project without a way to ask for what I need?

He decides the next client won't get a loose "I'll just need the content from you." They will get something warm and clear, in writing, from day one.
