Photos Delivered, Payment Delayed? The Delivery Slot

2026-10-25

The slot between eight and twelve

The sofa-bed is supposed to arrive between 8 and 12. It is 10:40. Mira is in her hallway in socks, laptop balanced on the shoe rack, refreshing two things at once.

The delivery app says driver is 14 stops away.

The banking app says nothing at all.

Two weeks ago she sent the gallery for a small brand shoot: forty edited images, colours matched, a few extra retouches she never mentioned. The client wrote back within the hour. These are gorgeous, thank you so much!! Three exclamation marks. A heart.

The final invoice went out the same afternoon. Since then, silence on the money. Not on the photos. The photos are already on their website. She saw them this morning, in a banner, bigger than she'd ever have chosen.

"This week, definitely"

The first nudge got a cheerful answer: Oh, so sorry, finance does these in batches, should be this week!

The second, a week later: Thanks for the reminder, I'll check with accounting.

Now it's a third week, and Mira has the draft open. It says, Just a quick follow-up on, and then the cursor blinks, because every ending sounds either too soft or too sharp.

She isn't angry. That's the odd part. She likes this client. She just feels a small, hot embarrassment, as if asking for the money she's owed were rude. As if the work were a favour that she's now putting a price on after the fact.

So she closes the draft. Again.

What the waiting costs

Nobody puts this on the invoice, but waiting has a price.

It's the way you don't quite relax on a Saturday. The way you say yes to a cheap job you'd normally decline, because just in case. The way you glance at your phone in the supermarket queue when it buzzes, and it's only a voucher offer.

It's also the fog. Was the deposit paid on this one? Did you agree 14 days or 30? Was it the balance of 600 or 450, after the extra hour you gave away? You could find out. It's all somewhere: an email thread, a note on your phone, a PDF named final_v2. But finding out feels like a small project, and so you don't, and the fog stays.

That's the thing about a late payment from someone who's friendly. It's hard to be firm with a smile. So you don't say anything, and the silence grows to fill the space.

The doorbell, finally

At 11:25 the bell rings. Two men, one sofa-bed, a lot of apologies about the stairwell.

While they carry it in, Mira catches herself apologising too: no, no, it's fine, take your time. Then she hears it, and nearly laughs. She's been saying no rush, it's fine to everyone this month. To the driver, to the client, to herself.

But here's what sticks. The delivery had a slot. A date. A tracking line that told her 14 stops away, then 6, then you're next. Waiting for it was uncomfortable, but it was never murky. She always knew where she stood.

Her money has no tracking line. That's the real problem. Not that the client is slow, but that she can't see anything. No due date she can point to. No picture of what's open, what's paid, what's been quietly sitting for twenty-one days.

It doesn't have to feel like begging

Later, with the sofa-bed unwrapped and the hallway smelling of cardboard, she opens a fresh sheet instead of the email draft.

One row per client. The shoot date. The deposit received. The gallery sent. The balance, the due date, the status.

It isn't clever. It takes her maybe ten minutes with the old threads open beside it. But when she's done, one cell sits there in plain colour: overdue, 9 days past due date. Not maybe. Not I think. A fact.

And something loosens. She doesn't have to feel like the pushy one. She can simply write, calmly: the invoice from the 4th was due on the 18th. Could you let me know when I can expect it? A date and a question. No apology, no drama. The email takes two minutes, not two weeks.

The feeling in her chest isn't triumph. It's quieter than that. It's the sense of a business that has a floor under it.

A different Monday

Picture the Monday after. Coffee, window open. Mira looks at one page: three shoots upcoming, one gallery due Thursday, two balances open, one of them flagged for a friendly nudge today.

No digging through threads. No guessing. She sends the nudge, closes the laptop, and goes to edit the next set with her full attention, because part of her isn't busy watching the banking app.

The money may still arrive on its own timetable. Clients are people, finance departments are slow. But she knows what she's waiting for, and since she can see it, she can stop carrying it around in her head.
