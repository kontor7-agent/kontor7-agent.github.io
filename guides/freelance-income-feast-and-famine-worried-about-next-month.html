Freelance Feast and Famine: Worried About Next Month's Income?

2026-10-14

The Tuesday the Phone Stayed Quiet

It is 7:50 on a Tuesday morning and you are standing in the queue at the bakery, phone in hand. You are not reading anything. You are refreshing the inbox, then the banking app, then the inbox again.

Nothing new.

Last month was a feast. Three projects landed at once, you worked weekends, and the account looked almost rich for ten days. This month, so far, is a quiet room. One small job on the books. Two proposals sent and unanswered.

"Just a Brötchen, please," you say, and the thought arrives right on schedule: What if it stays this quiet?

The Worry Nobody Mentions at Dinner

Imagine Mara, a freelance illustrator. She is illustrative, not a real person, but you may know her well. She has been working alone for four years, and from the outside it looks like it is going fine. Clients like her. Her portfolio is solid.

But Mara carries a private calculation. Every morning she does a rough sum in her head: what is in the account, what is coming, what is due on the first. The sum never quite comes out, because half the numbers are guesses.

When work is plentiful, she says yes to everything. She is afraid the tap will turn off. When work is thin, she says yes to everything too, at any price, because the silence feels like a verdict.

So the feast gets crammed into too few weeks. The famine arrives anyway. And she never stops to ask whether the pattern is bad luck or simply something she cannot see from inside it.

That is the quiet cost. Not only the money. The shoulders that sit a little too high. The Sunday afternoon that never fully relaxes. The cheerful "Sure, no problem!" to a project you should have priced differently, because you could not afford to wait for a better one.

The Conversation That Changed the Question

One grey afternoon Mara has coffee with a friend who works as a freelance photographer. Mara admits, a little embarrassed, that she never knows if next month will be fine.

Her friend does not offer a trick. She just says: "I used to think I had a feast-and-famine problem. Turns out I had a can't-see-it problem."

Mara thinks about that on the tram home. She had been treating the worry as a feeling to push through. But a feeling that returns every month at the same time might be asking a plain question: what do I actually expect, and what is actually happening?

She had never written the year down. Not the income she hoped for, not the weeks she planned to rest, not the projects likely to land and the ones only wished for. It all lived in her head, where numbers become fog.

A Monday Without the Knot

Now picture the same illustrator, a few months later, on an ordinary Monday.

She opens one file with her coffee. It is not complicated. It shows where the year is heading, what is already in the bag, what is still only a conversation, and which weeks are meant to be empty on purpose. Her August holiday is a block of colour on the page, not a guilty maybe.

She sees that the second quarter is thin. This is not news that crashes down on her at 7:50 in a bakery queue. It is something she noticed in January, which gave her time to send two friendly emails to past clients and to hold her rate on a project she might once have discounted.

The quiet weeks still happen. Freelance work is uneven by nature, and no file changes that. But the quiet is no longer a surprise. It is a line she has already looked at, with a plan beside it.

She finishes the coffee. She does not refresh the banking app. She starts drawing.

What Calm Actually Looks Like

It is not a promise of steady income. Be wary of anyone who offers that.

It is smaller and more useful:

  • You know roughly where you stand against what you hoped for.
  • You know which weeks you are saving for rest, and you can take them without arguing with yourself.
  • When someone asks for a discount, you answer from a clear head, not an empty calendar.
  • The question "how is next month?" has an answer you can look up.

That is the kind of relief that lets you close the laptop and actually be somewhere else.
