Small Business AI Prompts: The Job Ad You Kept Postponing

2026-10-15

It is Tuesday, 14:10. The lunch rush at the plant shop is over and the door has not chimed for twenty minutes.

You have a cup of tea going cold next to the till. On the laptop, a document called Saturday helper – ad has been open since last Thursday. It has one line in it: "We are looking for."

That is all. Six days, one line.

Let's call you Mara. You are an illustrative character, but you may know her. She runs the shop alone four days a week. She can tell a thirsty fern from a drowned one from across the room. And she is stuck on how to write a friendly sentence about a part-time job.

The list behind the blinking cursor

The job ad is not the only thing waiting. It never is.

There is a customer email from yesterday. Someone's delivery arrived with a cracked pot, and you have read the message three times. You know you want to be kind and fair, and you also know you cannot afford to be careless. So you have not answered yet.

There is the supplier. The ceramic planters you ordered are late again, and you need to say so without sounding rude. You do not want to lose the good price.

There are twelve new plants in the back that need product descriptions. You wrote the first three beautifully. The other nine are just "nice green plant, easy."

And there is Monday's plan, which currently lives on a sticky note that says order, count, ???.

None of this is hard work. That is the odd part. Each task is ten minutes if you could just begin. But beginning means finding the words, and words are what you have the least of at 14:10 on a Tuesday, after a morning of talking to people.

What the blank page actually costs

It does not cost money in a way you can point to. It costs smaller things.

It costs the evening, because the email you could not write at two o'clock gets written at nine, tired, in a tone you would not choose.

It costs the Saturday help you need, because the ad is not live and you will be on your feet alone again.

It costs a little pride. You run a real business, with real customers and a real supplier. Yet some days you feel like someone winging it, rewriting the same polite sentence in your head while the tea goes cold.

You have heard that AI can write these things. You tried it once. You typed "write a job ad" and got four paragraphs of corporate fog: dynamic team, exciting opportunity, fast-paced environment. It sounded like a company you would never want to work for, and nothing like your shop with its three cats' worth of dust and its smell of wet soil.

So you closed the tab. The tool was not the problem. The question you asked it was too thin.

The small thing that changes the question

A few days later, a friend who runs a bike repair stand mentions it in passing, over the fence of a market stall. She does not push an app. She says she stopped asking AI for "a reply" and started giving it a few blanks to fill in first: who the customer is, what happened, what she is willing to offer, how she wants to sound.

That is all she says. But it stays with you.

The fog came from not telling it anything. If it knew your shop, your tone, the cracked pot and the refund you are happy to give, it would not need to guess. And you would not need to start from zero. You would only need to fill in a few brackets.

It does not have to be like this. Not every email needs to be a small crisis.

A different Tuesday

Picture the same 14:10, a few weeks on.

The door is quiet again. The tea is still warm, because you have not been staring at anything.

You open the job ad and fill in the brackets: Saturdays, 9 to 15, small plant shop, someone who likes talking to people and does not mind carrying soil. A draft appears that sounds like you. You change two words, delete a sentence that is too grand, and it is done. You read it once, as you always would, because the final say is yours.

The customer with the cracked pot gets a warm, clear reply in three minutes. The supplier gets a firm, friendly note about the delay. The nine plants get nine descriptions with their own small personality.

Then you write the plan for Monday, and it is no longer order, count, ???

Nothing about the AI is magic. You still check every line, and you still decide what to offer and what to say. It simply means you are editing instead of starting from nothing. Editing is the part you are good at.

By 15:00 you have time to re-pot the ficus that has been looking at you sideways all week.

That is what you get back: not hours of free time, just the knot in the stomach loosening a little, and your evening staying yours.
