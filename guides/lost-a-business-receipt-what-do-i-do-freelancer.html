Lost a Business Receipt as a Freelancer? The Blank Slip

2026-11-03

Wednesday, 12:40. Lena has a sandwich in one hand and a crumpled strip of paper in the other.

She is a freelance illustrator, sitting on a bench outside a café with her coat still on. The paper came out of her coat pocket while she was looking for her keys. It is the receipt for the monitor she bought in March. It cost 340 euros, and it is completely blank.

Not torn. Not stained. Just white. The ink has faded away, the way thermal paper does, and all that is left is a faint grey ghost of a logo.

She turns it over, as if the other side might have kept something. It hasn't.

The thought that arrives before the sandwich is finished

Great. Now what?

Then the second thought, the one nobody says out loud: A real business would have this sorted.

She is nine years into working alone, and part of her still feels like she is winging it. The monitor is not a luxury. She uses it eight hours a day. But the proof that she bought it has just vanished, and her mind goes straight to the worst corner.

What else is missing? There was the train ticket to the client workshop. The font licence. The two lunches with an art director that she is fairly sure were business. They are somewhere. A bag, a drawer, an email she will never find again.

She puts the sandwich down. It's no longer interesting.

What a lost receipt really costs

The monitor is not really the problem. One missing slip is rarely the end of anything, and there are usually other ways to show a purchase happened: a bank entry, an order confirmation, an email from the shop. Her tax professional will know what they accept, and that is their call to make, not hers.

What bothers Lena is the feeling underneath. The quiet suspicion that her paperwork lives in a dozen places and she is the only one who knows where. That every January, she will be on the floor with a shoebox, a coffee gone cold, and a vague sense of shame.

She remembers last January. She remembers it very well. Three hours on a Sunday, sorting little pieces of paper into piles that made less and less sense. Her partner had asked if she wanted to go for a walk. She said she'd be done in a minute. She was not done in a minute.

That is the real price. Not the 340 euros. The Sunday.

The turn, on a bench

She sits there a while longer, watching a delivery cyclist argue with a parking sign.

Then a small, practical thought comes in, almost politely. The problem isn't this receipt. The problem is that I only ever meet my receipts when something goes wrong.

They arrive in her life as paper, as email, as a photo on her phone, as a vague memory of a card payment. They don't arrive anywhere in particular. There is no front door. So of course some of them slip out the back.

She doesn't need to become a different person. She doesn't need to be the kind of freelancer who colour-codes her pens. She just needs one place where each purchase lands, on the day it happens, with a date and an amount and a note. Ten seconds each. A place that also shows her, quietly, what is still missing before the year ends.

That's all. One door, instead of a dozen windows.

She finds the order confirmation for the monitor in her inbox before she has finished the thought. It takes forty seconds. The shop emailed it in March. It was there the whole time, waiting.

She exhales for the first time in ten minutes.

A different kind of Monday

Picture a Monday in the new year.

Lena makes tea. She opens one file. There is a list of the year's purchases, each with a date, an amount and a small tick beside it. A progress bar at the top is nearly full. The few gaps are marked in plain sight: train ticket, March, still to add. She adds it, in under a minute, from a bank entry.

No shoebox. No Sunday. No stomach drop when a slip turns out to be blank.

When her tax professional asks for her records, she doesn't feel the old wobble. She sends one tidy file and goes back to her drawing. The afternoon is still hers. The light on the desk is still good.

That is all she wanted. Not a perfect system. Just the feeling of having nothing to hide from herself.
