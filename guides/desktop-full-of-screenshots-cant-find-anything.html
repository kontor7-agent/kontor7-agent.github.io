Desktop Full of Screenshots? The Kitchen TV Mirror

2026-10-23

It's Wednesday, a little after eleven, and you are officially on holiday.

You're at your sister's house for a long weekend, a week "off" that is half off. The kitchen smells of toast. Her kids are lined up on the sofa waiting for the cartoon you promised to put on.

So you open your laptop, press the cast button, and the big TV on the wall lights up.

With your desktop.

All of it. In a size you have never seen it.

Two hundred little squares

There are screenshots called Screenshot 2026-09-14 at 16.22.07. There are screenshots called Screenshot (212). There is a file called final_final_v3_REAL.pdf, one called new folder (4), and a spreadsheet you can't place at all.

Your seven-year-old nephew reads one out loud, slowly, the way kids do. "Un-ti-tled."

Your sister laughs kindly. You laugh too, a beat late.

But your stomach has gone quiet in the way it does when something you've been ignoring is suddenly very visible.

Because your phone buzzes on the counter. A client. Could you resend the signed agreement before our 14:00 call? I can't find it on my side.

You know you have it. You saved it. Somewhere.

The search that eats the morning

The cartoon starts. You stay at the table, laptop angled away from the screen, and begin the hunt.

You type "agreement" into the search bar. Nine results, none of them it. You try "signed". Nothing useful. You open Downloads and it scrolls, and scrolls, a long grey river of files with names you didn't choose, because the websites chose them for you.

You open a PDF. Wrong client. You open another. Last year's.

The thought in your head is not really about the agreement. It's the quieter one underneath: this is what a real business looks like? Me, hunting through a river at half past eleven, on a day I promised myself I'd rest?

It's a small thing. That's the trouble with it. Nobody ever says "my desktop is ruining my week". You just lose twenty minutes here, forty there. You lose the feeling of being the person who has it together. You lose a little of the holiday, because part of your mind stays behind in the mess.

You find the agreement at 11:52, inside a folder called misc, inside another folder called misc old. You send it. Your hands are a bit damp.

Then you look up, and your sister is watching you over her coffee.

"Do you want me to say something," she says, "or just pass the toast?"

The thing nobody tells you about tidying

You have tried to fix this before, of course.

There was the Sunday you dragged everything into folders by hand. It felt wonderful for two days. By the following Friday the screenshots had come back, quietly, like weeds.

There was the afternoon you decided to rename everything properly, and got through eleven files before the phone rang.

The problem was never that you're messy. It's that tidying by hand is a job with no end, and you already have a job. And there's a small, sensible fear underneath: what if I move the wrong thing and can't get it back? So you leave it all exactly where it is.

Your sister, who knows nothing about file systems, says something that stays with you.

"Doesn't the computer do the boring bits for you? Mine sorts my photos."

You open your mouth to say it's more complicated than that. Then you close it.

Is it?

The idea that it could just sort itself

That evening, after the kids are in bed, you sit on the back step with your laptop and the garden going blue.

You don't want a big project. You want something small and dull that does the dull work: sorts the screenshots, gives the files names a human would choose, spots the duplicates, tucks the old stuff away. And, importantly, shows you what it's about to do before it does it, with a way to take it all back if you don't like it.

A safety net. A tidy-up that asks first.

You realise you've been treating the mess as a character flaw, when it's really just a chore nobody gave to a machine.

A normal Monday, after

Picture a Monday a few weeks on.

It's 8:50. Coffee is still hot. You open the laptop and your desktop is almost empty, a calm pale picture and three things you're actually working on.

A client messages: Can you send the agreement before our call? You type the client's name, and there it is. First try. Dated, named, sitting where agreements live. You attach it and send before the kettle clicks off.

No river. No misc old. No quiet dread when someone says "just share your screen."

When the call starts at 14:00, you share without thinking about it. Nobody reads an "Un-ti-tled" out loud. You notice, with mild surprise, that you feel like a professional who runs a proper business.

And it all happened in the background, while you got on with the work you're actually paid for.
