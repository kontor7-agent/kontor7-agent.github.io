Massage Therapist Pricing Confused? The Gift Voucher Ask

2026-11-02

It is Thursday, 12:40, and the room still smells of lavender oil and warm towels.

Mira is wiping down the table when the door opens a hand's width. A woman from her morning list is back, coat half on, phone in her hand.

"Sorry, quick question. I want to buy a voucher for my sister. Which is the better one, the 60 or the 90? What would you do?"

Mira smiles. She says something warm about how the 90 is lovely if you really want to switch off. The woman nods, thanks her and pays.

Then the door closes, and Mira stands there with the cloth in her hand.

Because she has no idea which one she would rather sell.

The answer you didn't have

She knows what she charges. The 60 is one price, the 90 is a bit more, and the hot stone add-on is a number she picked two years ago because it "felt about right". The deep tissue slot costs the same as the relaxation one, because changing it felt like starting an argument.

What she doesn't know is what any of it leaves her with.

The room is rented by the day. The oils are not free. Neither is the laundry, the booking app or the stones that need replacing. And the 90-minute slot eats a whole afternoon, with the changeover, the notes and the water she drinks standing at the sink. Does it earn more than two 60s? Or just feel more generous?

She sits on the stool and opens her notes app. It is a list of prices with a few crossed-out numbers beside them.

There is a small, familiar knot under her ribs. It is not panic, just a quiet feeling of I run a business and can't answer a simple question about it.

The part nobody says out loud

This is the worry that rarely makes it into conversation. You are busy. Your regulars rebook. Your week looks full. And still, at the end of the month, the money in the account is a little thinner than the diary suggests.

So you do what many people do. You keep your prices where they are, because raising them feels risky. You say yes to the awkward Friday-evening slot. You tell yourself that busy must mean fine.

But "busy" and "earning" are different things. Some services quietly carry the others. Some quietly cost you the afternoon. And when you can't see which is which, you can't say your prices with a calm voice. You say them with a little apology in it.

Mira has heard that apology in her own voice. Last week a client asked if the 90 was "worth it", and she said, "Well, it depends!" Then she changed the subject.

What she finally did on a quiet Sunday

Nothing dramatic. No big course, no overhaul.

On Sunday morning, with coffee and no clients, she sat down and put her services in one place. Each one got the same few honest questions: how long it really takes, what it uses up, what the room costs her for that stretch of the day.

It took less than an hour. And for the first time, the list looked back at her.

The 60-minute relaxation massage, her bread and butter, was fine. Better than fine. The hot stone add-on was earning quietly, almost embarrassingly well, and she had been nearly giving it away. The long 90-minute slot, the one she had been proud of, was not the star she'd imagined. It wasn't a disaster either. It simply needed a different price to match the afternoon it takes.

No shame in any of it. It was just information, and information is surprisingly kind. It turns a vague worry into a plain fact, and plain facts can be changed.

A normal Thursday, a different feeling

Now imagine the same hallway moment, a few weeks on.

The woman puts her head round the door. "Which voucher would you pick for my sister?"

Mira doesn't pause. "If she wants real time to unwind, the 90. If she's never had a massage, start with the 60. Either way, you can't go wrong."

Her answer is warm, and it is also true. She isn't recommending the longer slot to look generous, or the shorter one out of fear. She knows what each one does for the person on the table and for her own month.

The woman pays. The door closes. Mira refills the water jug, folds a towel and notices something odd: nothing in her chest.

No knot, no vague "I should look at my prices someday". Just a quiet room and an afternoon she understands.

That is what it feels like when you know what each service really earns. It isn't a spreadsheet moment. It is your own voice, steady, when someone asks you a simple question about your own work.
