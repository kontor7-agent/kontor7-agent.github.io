See Where Your Time Goes: Time Tracking for Freelancers

The client writes on a Thursday afternoon: "Could you send a quick breakdown of the hours on this one?" You open your calendar. You scroll through your sent mail. You try to remember whether that Tuesday call was forty minutes or ninety. Somewhere in your chest, a small knot tightens.

Most of us who work alone know this moment. The work was real. You were busy, maybe too busy. But when someone asks for the numbers, the days blur together. You guess. You round down, because guessing high feels risky. Later you wonder why the month felt so full and the account looks so thin.

That feeling has a name: not knowing where your time goes. It is quiet and it costs you. It shows up as projects that paid less than you thought, invoices that take an evening to piece together, and a vague sense that you are not running a "real" business.

What changes when this is handled is not dramatic. You jot down hours in one place, without fuss. When a client asks, you answer in a minute. When you quote a new job, you know what the last similar one really took. You can see which clients are worth your Tuesdays and which ones quietly eat them. And the evening that used to vanish into reconstructing the month stays yours.

You do not need a heavy system. You need a simple habit, a place to put it, and a few honest numbers: your billable hours, your real hourly rate, the time a meeting actually costs.
