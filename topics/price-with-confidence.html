Price with Confidence: Freelance Rates, Quotes & Raises

It is 23:00. The quote is half written, the cursor blinks next to the number, and you have deleted it three times. Too high and they will say no. Too low and you will resent the project by week two.

That knot is familiar to most people who work for themselves. You know your craft. What you do not always have is a number you can say out loud without your voice going small. So you guess, you compare yourself with strangers online, and you quietly round down. Later, on a long walk or in a slow month, the doubt comes back: am I undercharging?

This is not a character flaw. Most of us were never shown how a price is built. Nobody explains that your rate has to carry the unpaid hours, the quiet weeks, the admin, the holidays and the tax you will owe. Without that picture, any figure feels like a gamble.

When the picture is clear, something changes. The rate stops being a verdict on your worth and becomes the result of a few honest inputs. You can write a quote and send it before bedtime. You can add a buffer without apologising. You can look at a finished project, see what it actually paid per hour, and decide what to do differently next time. Saying no to a poor fit gets easier, because you know your floor.

This page gathers our best pieces for that. Start with a story if you want to feel less alone with the question, pick a calculator if you want a number today, or open the free template if you would like everything in one place. Take what helps. The decisions stay yours, and none of this replaces advice from a tax professional.
