Never Sold Out by Surprise: Stock Tracking for Small Shops

It is Saturday morning at the market. A customer picks up the last jar on your table, smiles, and asks if you have two more. You do not. Or you think you do. Your phone buzzes with an online order for the same item, and your stomach drops.

That small, hot feeling is not about one jar. It is the feeling of not quite knowing what is on your shelf. You sold something you could not deliver. Now you write the apologetic email, issue the refund, and wonder what else is quietly running low.

Most small shops do not run out because they are careless. They run out because the numbers live in too many places: a notebook, a spreadsheet that stopped keeping up, a memory of what you ordered last month. Stock moves faster than the notes do.

When this is handled, the shelf stops surprising you. You know roughly how many days of an item are left. You know the point at which it is time to reorder, with a little buffer for slow suppliers. You stop holding money in stock that barely moves, and you stop apologising to people who wanted to buy from you.

Nothing about this needs to be complicated. It needs one clear place to look and a few simple numbers you trust. The pieces below start with stories you may recognise, then move to free calculators you can use in your browser, and finally to a tracker if you want it all in one file.

Take whichever step fits where you are today. Even one honest look at your fastest-selling item is a good beginning. Calm stock is not about perfection. It is about no longer being caught off guard.
