FAQ

Why does unpaid prep time change my lesson rate?

Your students pay for the lesson, but you work for the lesson plus the prep and admin around it. If you charge only for the teaching hour, your real hourly pay is lower than it looks. The calculator shows both the rate per lesson and your real hourly pay including unpaid time.

What should I enter as my take-home income?

Enter the amount you want to keep each month after tax and business costs, the money you could spend on rent and groceries. The calculator adds your costs and tax set-aside on top to find the income you need to bill.

What percentage should I set aside for tax?

It depends on your country, your total income and your situation. The default of 25% is only a placeholder. Ask a tax professional or accountant what fits you, then change the field.

How many lessons per week should I enter?

Enter the paid lessons you can realistically teach in a normal week, not your absolute maximum. Look at your calendar or lesson dates from the last few months for an honest average. Cancellations and weeks off are handled in separate fields.

Should I charge the same rate for every student?

Not necessarily. Use the result as a baseline. Longer lessons, exam preparation, travel to a student or very short notice can reasonably cost more. Group lessons can be priced per person, as long as the total covers your time.

How do I keep track of lessons and payments?

A simple record of lesson dates, what was agreed and what has been paid is enough. You do not need any personal details beyond what you need to invoice. Check each month who still owes you for completed lessons, and send invoices promptly.
