Review Link on Roofing Invoice and Email Signature

You finish a tear-off in the afternoon, the homeowner shakes your hand and says, "I'll definitely leave you a review." Two weeks later the final invoice goes out, the check comes in, and there is still nothing on Google. The customer was not lying. They just had no easy next step.

Your invoice and your email signature are the two places where that next step can live. Every customer gets an invoice, and you send emails all week anyway. Here is how to set both up in an afternoon.

Why put a review link on an invoice at all?

Because the review is part of how the next homeowner finds you. According to Roofr's Homeowner Perspective Report (November 2025), 82% of homeowners read Google reviews before they call a roofer. BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey, which asked 1,002 US adults, found that Google is the most used review platform, named by 71% of consumers.

A link on paper or in an email removes the excuses: "I didn't know where to go" and "I forgot." The customer taps once and lands on your review box.

How do I get my Google review link?

Sign in to your Google Business Profile and look for the option to share a review form or ask for reviews. Google changes the wording of its menus now and then, so look for "Get more reviews" or a similar button. It gives you a short link that opens the review box for your business directly.

Before you use it:

  1. Open the link on your own phone, not just your computer.
  2. Check that it shows your company name, not a competitor with a similar one.
  3. Save it in a note so you can paste it anywhere.

If the link is long and ugly, a short, readable web address on your own site that redirects to it also works. Keep it simple enough to type if someone has to.

What should the invoice say?

Put the request near the bottom, under the totals and payment details, so it does not compete with the amount due. Two or three short lines are enough:

Thank you for choosing us for your new roof. If you were happy with the work, a short Google review helps other homeowners find us: [link]

A few practical points:

  • Use a QR code too. Paper invoices and printed final walk-through sheets get looked at on a kitchen counter. A QR code lets the customer point their phone at it. Print it big enough to scan, and test it.
  • Make the link clickable in PDFs. If you send invoices as PDFs from your accounting software, send yourself one and tap the link on a phone.
  • Put it on the paid receipt, not only the first invoice. Many roofers send a deposit invoice, then a final one. The best moment is when the customer sees the job is done and paid.
  • Do not tie it to payment. Never write anything that suggests the review affects the price, a warranty, or a discount. Google's policy says you must not pay for reviews.

The wording should sound like you. "Thanks again, it was a pleasure working on your home" beats a stiff block of text.

How do I add the link to my email signature?

Your signature goes out on every estimate, schedule change, and photo update, so it quietly reminds people for weeks. Keep it short:

Mike Reyes | Reyes Roofing (555) 555-0123 Happy with your roof? Leave us a Google review: [link]

Tips that save headaches:

  • Use a text link, not a big image. Images are often blocked or land in the attachments list, and they bloat the email.
  • Set it up for everyone. If your office manager, crew lead, and estimator all email customers, they all need the same signature. Otherwise the link only appears when you write.
  • Set it on your phone too. Most roofers answer email from a truck. Gmail and Outlook apps have their own signature settings, separate from the desktop version.
  • Keep the line friendly, not pushy. One line is plenty. A signature with three banners reads like an advertisement.

According to the 2024 Homeowner Roofing Survey in Roofing Contractor magazine, 95% of homeowners rate online reviews as extremely important, very important, or important when hiring a roofer. That is a good reason to treat a signature line as part of your normal work, not an extra.

Is a link on the invoice enough?

No. It is a standing reminder, not a personal request. A message that says "Thanks for trusting us with your roof, here is the link" works better when it arrives separately from the bill. Pair the invoice line and signature with a direct ask once the job is finished.

A few rules hold no matter how you send the link:

  • Ask every customer the same way. Do not pick only the ones you think are happy.
  • Never pay for reviews, and never write them yourself or have your crew write them.
  • If you send the link by text, check the current rules on texting customers first.

It also helps to keep the stream steady. BrightLocal's 2026 survey found that 74% of consumers look for reviews written in the last three months, so a link that is used on every job beats a burst of requests once a year.
