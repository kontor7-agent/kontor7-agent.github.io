Roofing Website Contact Form That Gets Leads

You are on a roof when your phone buzzes. Then it buzzes again. By the time you climb down, the homeowner has moved on to the next roofer on the list.

Many of those visitors never wanted to call. They wanted a simple way to say, "Can someone look at my roof?" Your website should give them exactly one way to do that.

This guide is about that one form: where it goes, how short it is, and what happens after someone presses send.

One roofing form beats five options

Look at your site on your phone. How many ways can a visitor reach you? A phone number, a "Contact" page, a newsletter box, a chat widget, a quote calculator.

Every extra option makes the visitor think. A homeowner with a leaking ceiling does not want to choose. They want to be told what to do next.

Pick one action: request an estimate. Keep the phone number visible for people who prefer to call. But make the form the main button everywhere else.

This matters because of how people find you. According to Roofing Contractor magazine's 2024 Homeowner Roofing Survey, 54% of homeowners use search engine results to find a roofing contractor. Many of them land on your site cold, with no one to guide them.

If you want to understand why some of them pick the form over the phone, read Why Homeowners Fill Out Roofing Forms at Night, Not Call.

Place the estimate form above the fold

"Above the fold" means visible before anyone scrolls. On a phone, that is a small space. Use it well.

Put these things there:

  • A plain headline, such as "Request a roof estimate"
  • One line saying who you serve, such as "Roof repair and replacement in [City] and nearby towns"
  • A button that opens the form or the form itself

Then repeat the same button in three more places: the top menu, the bottom of every service page, and the footer. Same words, same color, same form.

Do not send people to a separate "Contact Us" page with an address, a map, and a long paragraph about your history. They came to ask for an estimate. Let them.

A few other basics that cost nothing:

  • Make the button big enough for a thumb.
  • Make sure the form loads fast on a weak signal.
  • Test it on your own phone once a month.

Ask four things, then stop

The longer the form, the fewer people finish it. A roofer does not need a full inspection report before the first conversation.

For a short form, ask for these four things:

  1. Name
  2. Phone number or email (let them pick the one they prefer)
  3. Property address or ZIP code
  4. A short note: "What's going on with your roof?"

That is enough to call back and book a visit. Anything else you can ask on the phone or at the house.

For a deeper look at which questions help and which ones scare people off, see Roofing Estimate Request Form: What to Ask and Skip.

Two small touches help. Put a short line under the button, like "We reply the same business day." Only write that if you will keep the promise.

Also put a few real reviews near the form. Roofr's 2026 Homeowner Perspective Report found that 82% of homeowners checked online reviews before hiring a roofer. They are looking for proof right at the moment they decide.

According to Roofing Contractor magazine's 2024 survey, 68% of roofing contractors feature online reviews on their website. Put yours next to the form, not buried on a separate page.

Confirm the request on a thank-you page

The moment after someone presses send is where trust is won or lost. If the page just says "Submitted" and nothing else, the homeowner wonders if anything happened.

Use a thank-you message that does three jobs:

  • Says the request arrived
  • Says who will reply and roughly when
  • Tells them what to do if it is urgent

Here is a short example:

Thanks, [First name]. We got your request for [Street]. [Your name] will call or email you by [time, e.g. tomorrow noon]. If water is coming in right now, call [phone number].

Send a matching confirmation by email or text as well. Keep it short and send it right away.

Subject: We got your roof estimate request

Hi [First name],

Thanks for reaching out about your roof at [Street]. I'll contact you by [time] to set up a visit.

If it's urgent, call me at [phone number].

[Your name], [Company]

If you text homeowners, check the current rules on consent first. Keep it general: ask permission, and make it easy to opt out.

Reply fast, then ask for the review

A form only works if someone answers it. Decide who checks new requests, how often, and what they say first. On a small crew, that is often you, between jobs.

Set a rhythm, such as morning, lunch, and end of day. Our guide Roofing Lead Follow-Up: How Fast to Reply, What to Write covers the first reply in detail.

The form also feeds your reviews. A homeowner who had a smooth first contact is more likely to say so later. Ask every customer the same way after the job. Never pay for reviews, and never write them yourself.
