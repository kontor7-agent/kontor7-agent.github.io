Get Your First 20 Google Reviews as a Roofer

You finish a tear-off on a Friday. The homeowner shakes your hand and says, "I'll leave you a great review." Three weeks later your Google profile still shows nothing.

The homeowner didn't lie. They got busy, and you never made it easy. For a new roofing business, that gap is the whole problem.

Homeowners do check. According to Roofr's 2026 Homeowner Perspective Report, 82% of homeowners checked online reviews before hiring a roofer. An empty profile can lose you the job to a competitor with worse work and more stars.

Twenty reviews is a practical first goal. Here is how to get there.

Review link first, then the ask

Before you ask anyone, get your tools ready. Claim your Google Business Profile and copy the direct review link from it.

Open that link on your own phone. Make sure it lands on the star rating screen for your company, not a search page.

Save the link in your phone notes so you can paste it in ten seconds. If you have to hunt for it, you will skip the ask on busy days.

Want both emails as copy-paste text? They are at the end of this guide.

Past customers are your fastest start

You probably have more reviews waiting than you think. Write down every job you have done in the last two years, including small repairs.

Include the neighbors, the landlord with three properties, and the customer who sent you a thank-you note. Then send each one the same kind of message.

Google's rules matter here. Ask every customer the same way. Do not pick only the ones you think are happy. Never pay for reviews, and never write them yourself.

Here is a short text for a past customer:

Hi [First name], it's [Your name] from [Company]. Thanks again for trusting us with your roof on [Street]. If you have two minutes, a Google review would mean a lot to a small company like ours: [review link]. Honest feedback is welcome. Thank you.

Texting rules vary, so check the current rules before you message people in bulk. Email is a fine alternative for older jobs.

Ask in person within two days

For new jobs, the ask starts before you leave the driveway. Do the final walkthrough, point out the cleanup, and then say something like this:

"Before I head out, one thing. I'm building up the company, and Google reviews are how new customers find us. I'll send you a link tonight. Honest feedback is all I want."

Then send the message that same evening or the next morning. People forget fast, and the finished roof is most vivid right after the job.

We cover the timing in detail in Best Time to Ask for a Roofing Review After the Job.

A few habits make the ask work:

  • Ask the person who made the decision, not the tenant or the neighbor.
  • Use their first name and the street, so it feels personal.
  • Send one link, not three platforms to choose from.
  • Say thank you whether or not they post.

One reminder, then leave it alone

Most people who intend to write a review need one nudge. Send it about five days after the first message.

Keep it short and free of guilt. Here is an email you can adapt:

Subject: Quick favor about your roof on [Street]

Hi [First name],

I hope the new roof is holding up well. If you haven't had a chance yet, a short Google review would help other homeowners find an honest roofer: [review link].

If something about the job wasn't right, reply to this email and I'll call you.

Thanks, [Your name], [Company]

That last line matters. It gives an unhappy customer a way to reach you first, without screening anyone out of the request. Everyone still gets the same email.

After the reminder, stop. A third message turns a friendly ask into pressure. For more wording options, see How to Ask Roofing Customers for a Google Review.

Keep the pace after review twenty

Hitting twenty is not the finish line. BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey found that 74% of consumers look for reviews written in the last three months. A burst of reviews followed by silence will look stale.

The fix is to make the ask part of every job. Put it on your closing checklist, right next to the final invoice.

Referrals work the same way. A neighbor's recommendation sends people to your name, and your Google profile is what they find next. Roofing Contractor magazine's 2024 Homeowner Roofing Survey found that 73% of homeowners go by word of mouth or a recommendation.
